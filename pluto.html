1. pluto

Pluto was discovered in 1930 and was originally considered the ninth planet in the Solar System. In 2006, it was reclassified as a dwarf planet because it does not fully clear its orbital path.

2. Surface and Orbit

Pluto is located in the Kuiper Belt, a region filled with icy objects beyond Neptune. Its surface is covered with ice, mountains, and frozen plains. Pluto has a very unusual orbit that is more oval-shaped compared to the orbits of major planets.

3. Exploration and Discovery

In 2015, NASA’s New Horizons spacecraft became the first mission to visit Pluto. It captured detailed images that showed mountains made of ice and complex surface features. Pluto continues to interest scientists because it provides important information about the outer regions of the Solar System.

4. Pluto and the Kuiper Belt

Although Pluto is no longer officially classified as a major planet, it remains one of the most fascinating objects in the Solar System. Pluto is now considered a dwarf planet because it does not clear other objects from its orbital path. It is located in a distant region called the Kuiper Belt, which contains many icy bodies and small celestial objects.

5. Discoveries of New Horizons Spacecraft

Pluto became much more interesting after NASA’s New Horizons spacecraft flew past it in 2015. The mission revealed mountains made of ice, frozen plains, and a surprisingly complex surface. Scientists also discovered that Pluto has a thin atmosphere that changes as the dwarf planet moves around the Sun. These discoveries showed that even small and distant worlds can be active and scientifically important.
